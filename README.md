# US_Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics_Data_Analysis
Data analysis project using real data from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics to gain an in-depth insight into actual occupational trends and job growth.
Tech Stack: JupyterLab | Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Numpy)

                                                                  Executive Summary
  The job market in the United States of America has been in a state of tension for a few years. There has been a tremendous amount of speculation that artificial intelligence is the cause of mass layoffs and a reduction in new job opportunities. Although artificial intelligence has had a significant impact on the way many companies view productivity and get things done, the reality of the economy is much more complex than the simplistic argument that AI is taking people's jobs. In order to get a better understanding of what is actually happening, I gathered real data from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics.
  Part 1 of my analysis covers 'Table 1.3 Fastest growing occupations, 2025 and projected 2035 (Employment in thousands)'. Before looking at the economy as a whole, it makes sense to take a look at the fastest growing jobs to highlight the disparity between the idea that the job market is shrinking and the actuality of more jobs being added in certain fields. Using Python to visualize the data and crunch the numbers, I was able to get a better perspective of what is happening. These are the key takeaways.
  
  - Excluding positions associated with therapy, jobs that fall under the umbrella of healthcare account for 42.69% of median wages earned amongst the fastest growing occupations while jobs that fall under computer science and information technology account for 21.73% of median wages earned.
  - Home health and personal care aides account for the most jobs in 2025 and will continue to hold that title through 2035. These jobs will have the most growth in total.
  - Nurse Practitioners, Solar Photovoltaic Installers, Data Scientists, and Wind Turbine Service Technicians are projected to grow the most by 2035 percentagewise. This signifies an increase in demand and velocity of job creation for these particular jobs. 

  There are a few conclusions that can be derived from this relatively brief analysis. Healthcare opportunities show no signs of slowing down. I posit that this can be attributed to the highly regulated nature and heavy emphasis on the implicitly human trait of empathy associated with the healthcare field. The demand increases for Solar Photovoltaic Installers and Wind Turbine Service Technicians indicate a strong correlation to the environmental need and social desire to implement green technology in our society. Last, but certainly not least, the demand for Data Scientists and higher wages for computer science and IT jobs are directly tied to advancements in artificial intelligence and the computing infrastructure of the world at large.
